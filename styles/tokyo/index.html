01 — About

Many simple parts, interacting locally, produce behavior that none of them contains.

Ant colonies, markets, cities, language: systems that compute without anyone doing the computing. I build socio-economic models populated by LLM-driven agents and watch what institutions, norms, and failures emerge. Before research: advertising, and a feature film script written and sold. A model is a story you can run; a story is a model you can feel.

02 — Research
001

Emergent computation

The conditions under which collective behavior becomes information processing — cellular automata, swarms, economies.

002

LLM agents in socio-economic models

Language-model agents that negotiate, deceive, cooperate, and rationalize — laboratories for institutions, markets, and norms.

003

Systems that deserve to exist

Mechanism and policy design that is a net positive for the people inside it, honest about what models cannot tell us.

03 — Stories

People don't adopt ideas — they adopt stories that carry them.

Years in advertising taught me that; a sold feature film script remains one of my favorite empirical results. Patron saints: Terry Pratchett, whose Discworld is secretly a complex-systems textbook, and Ursula K. Le Guin, who treated every society as a thought experiment and every thought experiment as a moral one.

04 — Ethos
  • Net positive? Better off, including those who never get a vote.
  • Honest at the boundaries? Clear where the model stops being trustworthy.
  • Legible to the governed? The rules understandable by the people inside.
  • The Weatherwax test. Sin is treating people as things.
