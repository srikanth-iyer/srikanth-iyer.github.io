About

As a researcher I work on complex systems — the study of how many simple parts, interacting locally, produce behavior that none of them contains. Ant colonies, markets, cities, language: systems that compute without anyone doing the computing. My current work builds socio-economic models populated by LLM-driven agents, and watches what institutions, norms, and failures emerge.

It's part science, part world-building — which suits me, because before research I spent years in advertising learning how stories move people, and I've written (and sold) a feature film script.

Research

Emergent computation

The conditions under which collective behavior becomes information processing — in cellular automata, swarms, and economies alike.

LLM agents in socio-economic models

Language-model agents that negotiate, deceive, cooperate, and rationalize — turning agent-based models into laboratories for institutions, markets, and norms.

Systems that deserve to exist

Mechanism and policy design that is a net positive for the people inside it — honest about what models can and cannot tell us.

Stories

In advertising I learned that people don't adopt ideas — they adopt stories that carry them. A sold feature film script remains one of my favorite empirical results.

Patron saints: Terry Pratchett, whose Discworld is secretly a complex-systems textbook observed with furious kindness, and Ursula K. Le Guin, who treated every society as a thought experiment and every thought experiment as a moral one.

Ethos

  • Net positive? — better off, including those who never get a vote.
  • Honest at the boundaries? — clear where the model stops being trustworthy.
  • Legible to the governed? — rules understandable by the people inside.
  • The Weatherwax test — sin is treating people as things.
